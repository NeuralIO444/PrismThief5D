# PrismThief5D — a 5D Mark II scan workroom

**Spec · 2026-10-16 · status: approved to spec, no build yet**

## Premise

Latent Studios' The Ritual (@latentritual, Habib Saleh) got the shape right:
a photographic workroom for Mac and iPad built on three verbs — **gather,
develop the negative, make the print**. Film in, print out, nothing in
between that doesn't serve the frame.

PrismThief5D is our own version of that workroom, built on open-source
pieces and centered on the camera Matt already owns: the **Canon EOS 5D
Mark II**. Not a clone — The Ritual is a polished consumer app. Ours is a
working tool tuned to one body, one photographer's stocks, and one machine
(a Mac Studio). The 5D II is not a compromise body for this job: it is one
of the best-supported classic DSLR-scan bodies in existence, and its files
are the ones this pipeline is built around.

Why build it instead of buying the app or a scanner:

- **His camera.** The 5D II already sits in the bag next to the Pentax
  Super Program that exposes the film. One body shoots the scan; no new
  scanner hardware, no flatbed's fixed optics.
- **His stocks.** The develop stage is calibrated per roll, against the
  stocks he actually shoots — Tri-X 400, CineStill 800T, Fujichrome
  Astia 100, Ultramax 400 — not a generic "film look" preset pack.
- **His machine.** Everything runs locally on the Mac Studio. RAWs never
  leave the desk. The workroom is a pipeline he owns end to end, and can
  change when it annoys him — that's the creative-technologist point of
  the exercise.

The unit of work is **the roll**. Not the frame, not the folder — the
roll. Every stage below is organized around a roll session, the way a
darkroom session always was.

## The three stages

### 1 · GATHER — tethered capture, roll by roll

The pull half. **gphoto2 / libgphoto2** (gphoto/gphoto2, ★904) tethers
the 5D II over USB: trigger capture, auto-download the CR2, drive live
view for framing and focus. Wrappers exist for both of Matt's stacks —
python-gphoto2 (★429) and node-gphoto2 (★301); the pipeline language is
an open question below, the capability is not.

Pipeline steps:

1. **Open a roll session.** Name it (date + stock + camera, e.g.
   `2026-10-tri-x-super-program`), set the expected frame count (36/24/12),
   pick the stock profile. The session is a directory; everything the roll
   produces lives in it.
2. **Frame with live view.** The 5D II on the copy stand, film holder on
   the light pad, live view on the Studio screen. Focus check at 100% on
   a frame's grain, not its edges.
3. **Capture frame by frame.** Advance the holder, fire from the keyboard.
   Each CR2 lands in the session directory named by frame number
   (`f01.cr2`, `f02.cr2`…). Missed/blank frames are marked, not deleted —
   the roll record stays honest.
4. **Capture the rebate.** One extra frame per roll: the unexposed film
   border (the rebate strip, frame edges and all). This is not scrap —
   it's the develop stage's calibration sample (see below). Gather isn't
   done until the rebate frame exists.
5. **Close the roll.** Session manifest written: stock, body, lens,
   capture settings, frame map. The roll is now a sealed unit the other
   two stages can trust.

### 2 · DEVELOP — the negative, inverted honestly

The develop half, and the deep water. Reference points, all open source:
**amoslu-photo/simple-inversion** (★30) for camera-scan RAW inversion,
**Signynt's darkroom script** (★33) for inversion plus dust/scratch
cleanup, and **darktable's negadoctor** as the reference engine to study
— its orange-mask removal and per-channel film-curve model are the
standard this stage is judged against.

Pipeline steps:

1. **Decode the RAW.** LibRaw under the hood (via the chosen wrapper) —
   linear, no baked white balance, no tone curve. The negative is data
   until we say otherwise.
2. **Sample the film base.** From the rebate frame: read the unexposed
   base color per channel. For color negative this is the orange mask,
   measured from *this roll*, not assumed from a datasheet.
3. **Invert.** Per-channel inversion normalized against the sampled base
   (this is the negadoctor move): base-neutralize first, then invert,
   then apply per-channel film curves to place the tonal scale. B&W takes
   the simpler path — no mask to remove, so base sample sets black point
   and a single curve does the rest. That's why B&W ships first
   (Slice 1).
4. **Contact sheet.** The whole roll, inverted, in roll order, at a
   glance. This is the workroom's home screen and the photographer's
   first edit: pick frames from the sheet, the way it was always done
   with a loupe — except the sheet is honest positives, not negatives
   squinted at backwards.
5. **Per-frame finish.** Crop to the frame lines, straighten, set black/
   white points per frame where the roll-level curve isn't enough. Edits
   are non-destructive: stored as parameters against the CR2, re-rendered
   on demand, never baked.
6. **Dust pass (software only).** Signynt-style cleanup on the inverted
   frame. See Honest Limits — this is a helper, not a scanner's infrared
   channel.

### 3 · PRINT — out the door, print-ready

"Make the print" in The Ritual's terms; in ours, export that a lab (or a
home printer) can't get wrong.

Pipeline steps:

1. **Presets, not dialogs.** A small fixed set: lab-print TIFF (full-res,
   16-bit, embedded profile), share JPEG (long edge 2048, sRGB), archive
   master (the developed frame at full res, lossless). Pick the preset,
   the roll or the picked frames render.
2. **Print sizes honest to the file.** Presets carry target sizes (4×6,
   5×7, 8×10) with the effective DPI computed and shown — see the
   resolution math in Honest Limits. No upscaling theater.
3. **Roll-complete export.** The contact sheet exports too, as a sheet —
   the roll's index card, filed with the negatives.

| Print export presets | **Build** (thin) | Ours, over standard image writers |
| UI shell | **Build, last** | Slice 3 — pipeline first, face second |

## Honest limits

- **No infrared dust channel.** Dedicated film scanners see dust and
  scratches in an infrared pass the emulsion is transparent to. A DSLR
  scan has no such channel — cleanup is software inference on the visible
  image only. It will help; it will not be a Nikon Coolscan. Badly
  scratched rolls stay a retouching job.
- **Color is the deep end.** Negadoctor is years of tuning by people who
  think in film curves for a living. PrismThief5D v1 does not match it.
  V1 color = per-roll base calibration (the rebate sample) + good-enough
  per-channel curves, judged by eye against the stock. It will be honest
  and improvable roll over roll; it will not be finished. Anyone who
  promises one-click perfect CineStill is selling something.
- **Resolution math (35mm on the 5D II).** The 5D II sensor is
  5616 × 3744 px across a 36 × 24 mm frame → ≈ 156 px/mm ≈ **3,960 dpi
  effective** — comfortably past the ~2,400–3,200 dpi a good flatbed
  actually resolves, and enough for a sharp 12×18 print at ~300 dpi from
  the full frame with room to crop. The ceiling is the taking lens and
  holder flatness, not the body.
- **Not yet owned.** The pipeline assumes a capture rig Matt hasn't
  confirmed on hand: a **copy stand** (or tripod column that truly locks
  parallel), a **high-CRI light pad** (CRI 95+, even across the frame —
  a cheap pad's falloff becomes the develop stage's problem), and a
  **film holder** that holds the strip flat and shows the rebate. Small
  money, but it's a needs list, not an assumption.
- **One body, one format.** 35mm only, 5D II only, until the slices below
  are done. The R6 II and medium format are later questions, not v1 scope.

## Build slices

Evening work (7 PM–10 AM), pipeline before UI, each slice end-to-end
before the next begins.

- **Slice 1 — B&W, end to end.** Tether one roll of Tri-X through
  Gather (session, frame map, rebate frame), Develop on the simple path
  (base-as-black-point, single curve), and out comes an inverted contact
  sheet. No orange mask in B&W means no place for the pipeline to lie to
  itself — the fastest honest win, and it proves Gather + the roll model
  before color gets a vote.
- **Slice 2 — Color.** The full develop path on a roll of CineStill 800T:
  rebate base sampling, mask removal, per-channel curves. Judged by eye,
  roll over roll. Astia and Ultramax profiles follow as they're shot.
- **Slice 3 — The workroom shell.** The three stages get their face: one
  window, roll list → contact sheet → frame, print presets on the wall.
  Only after the pipeline underneath is boring and trusted.

## The Lois pass

George, on PrismThief5D. Blunt, as ordered.

**Rule 1 — Creativity solves problems.** The problem is real: film shot,
nowhere honest to develop it. This solves it with a camera he already
owns. Good. But the minute the software starts "enhancing," it stops
solving and starts decorating. The algorithm serves the eye or it dies.

**Rule 2 — Defeat habit.** The habit is the flatbed scanner — slow,
fixed, dumb glass from 2009. A 21-megapixel body on a stand defeats it.
Second habit to kill: the preset pack. "Film look" filters are a
costume. This spec samples the actual film base off the actual roll.
That's not a look. That's the negative telling the truth.

**Rule 3 — The nanosecond.** Contact sheet, whole roll, one glance. If
the sheet doesn't read instantly, the workroom failed. Everything else
is plumbing.

**Rule 4 — Assume smart.** No wizard. No "Scan My First Roll!" tour. The
man has shot Tri-X for years — the tool should talk to him like it.
Presets, not dialogs. Correct.

**Rule 5 — Cautious or creative.** One hedge, named: "good-enough
curves" for color. Fine — it's honest about v1. But don't let
good-enough become the permanent address. CineStill will expose cowardice
in the curves faster than any critic.

**Rule 6 — Work is worship.** A roll at a time. Session by session. The
tool rewards the man who shows up with another exposed roll. That's the
whole game.

**Rule 7 — No trends.** The trend is AI "film emulation" — type a prompt,
get grain. Garbage. This is the opposite: real film, real base sample,
real curves. Keep it that way. The day an "AI enhance" button appears,
burn the repo.

**Rule 8 — Discovered, not created.** The rebate frame is the discovery —
the calibration was sitting in the film border all along, and every
scanner threw it away. Build around what the negative already knows.

**Rule 9 — True to self.** It's named PrismThief and it develops *his*
stocks off *his* camera. Not a product. A workbench. Amplify the eye,
impose nothing.

**Kill list:** the AI enhance button (pre-emptively). Auto-everything.
The settings maze. Any "film look" preset with a city's name on it.

**Verdict:** A tool, not a toy. Build Slice 1, shoot a roll, and let the
contact sheet argue for Slice 2.
